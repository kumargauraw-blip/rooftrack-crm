Fix before you send

Four of these came out of reading the send path in server/routes/campaigns.js and server/lib/email.js. The first two will bite you.

Blocker Which phone number is right?

Your concept copy says 817-809-7000. Every template already in the CRM says (817) 966-2863. I used your number throughout, but 466 people are about to get it — confirm it rings, and that whoever answers knows this campaign went out.

Blocker Fill in the physical address in the footer

Nothing in the send path adds an unsubscribe link or a mailing address — I checked. CAN-SPAM requires both on commercial email, and their absence is also what tips a 466-recipient blast into spam folders. I built a reply-based opt-out and an address line into the template; replace [STREET ADDRESS], [CITY], TX [ZIP] before sending.

Check One record still has no name

The greeting renders {{first_name}} verbatim, so the record imported as UNNAMED - see IMG_2144.HEIC would go out as "Hi UNNAMED,". Read the name off that photo, or drop that recipient from the campaign.

Check Is $100 still the referral payout?

I carried it over from the referral template already in your CRM. If it's changed, it appears once in the email and once in the plain-text version.

Check Send a test to yourself first, then consider batching

Every send BCCs dennis@honestroof.com, so this campaign puts 466 copies in your own inbox — expect that. If the domain hasn't sent bulk mail before, 466 at once can hurt your sender reputation; splitting into two or three sends across a couple of days is safer.

Social

    
Same two beats everywhere: don't sign anything, then free honest look, 817-809-7000. The warning is what gets shared; the offer is what converts. Lead with the warning.


    

      

        Nextdoor — post here first
        
      

      

        
Better platform than Facebook for this exact message: hyper-local, and "who are these roofers" is already the most active thread type on it after a storm. Search your own neighborhood feed for existing threads and reply there too — warmer than a cold post. Nextdoor punishes anything that reads like an ad, so this one is written to stand on its own as a PSA: a neighbor could act on it, never call you, and it still did its job.

      

      

    


    

      

        Facebook — main organic post
        
      

      

      

        
On the closing line. An explicit "share this" instruction meaningfully beats hoping people do it unprompted — and here it happens to be true, which is why it doesn't read as a growth hack.

      

    


    

      

        Boosted / paid — short version
        
      

      

      

        
Targeting: homeowners, 10–15 miles around the hardest-hit ZIPs. Your customer base concentrates in Arlington, Fort Worth, Grand Prairie and Mansfield — weight toward wherever the storm actually tracked.

        
Suppression: exclude existing customers from paid targeting. They're getting the email; paying to reach them twice burns budget.

      

    


    

      

        Yard sign / truck magnet / graphic
        
      

      

      

        
Your original line, barely touched — the caps and the bluntness are the brand. Set DON'T SIGN ANYTHING as the largest element: it's the line that stops someone, and the only one that has to be readable from a moving car.

      

    


    

      

        SMS — optional, opt-in only
        
      

      

      

        
Only to customers who have texted you before or explicitly opted in. Cold bulk SMS carries real TCPA exposure in a way email does not — this is the one channel where the downside is a lawsuit rather than a spam folder.
